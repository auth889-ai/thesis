## RQ1 — Forecasting transfer to Dhaka: how far ahead, and why Dhaka differs

*Group: ML. City and data: Los Angeles, San Francisco Bay Area, Dhaka. Summary: `results/forecasting/rq1/summary.json`.*

**Question.** How well can traffic speed be forecast 15, 30 and 60 minutes ahead, in LA and in Dhaka, and why does Dhaka differ?

**Data.**
- **METR-LA** (Los Angeles, 207 loop detectors, 2012-03-01 to 2012-06-27) and **PEMS-BAY** (San Francisco Bay Area, 325 detectors, 2017-01-01 to 2017-06-30). Both from the DCRNN release (Li et al., ICLR 2018), in mph.
- **DhaTraX v1.0** (Dhaka, CC BY 4.0): 5-minute median speed in km/h at three camera sites, one recorded day each (BS 2026-04-06, INT 2026-04-19, BM 2026-04-20). The authors did not validate the calibration.

**Models and licences** (`third_party/SOURCES.md`, "Forecasting comparison, RQ1"):
- **Baselines:** persistence; the historical average by time of day; the historical average by time of day and weekday (from the train split).
- **Classical:** random forest and XGBoost.
- **Zero-shot:** Chronos-Bolt-small, Chronos-2 (one road at a time, and all roads together) and TimesFM 2.5, all Apache-2.0.
- **Trained** with the BasicTS harness (Apache-2.0): DCRNN, GWNet, MegaCRN, STAEformer, STID and D2STGNN on METR-LA; GWNet, STID and DCRNN on PEMS-BAY.

**Reproduced first** (`results/upstream/REPRODUCTIONS.json`, `results/forecasting/patchstg_largest.json`, `evidence/forecasting/reproduce/SUMMARY.json`):
- **Graph WaveNet, METR-LA** (MAE@15min): published 2.69 (STAEformer, Liu et al. CIKM 2023, Table 1 (retrained baselines)), ours 2.719: REPRODUCTION_PASS.
- **Graph WaveNet, PEMS-BAY** (MAE@15min): published 1.3 (STAEformer, Liu et al. CIKM 2023, Table 1 (retrained baselines)), ours 1.3083: REPRODUCTION_PASS.
- **OpenCity, cross-city (zero-shot)** (MAE (zero-shot, PEMS07M, authors' 288-step horizon)): published 4.5 (OpenCity, Li et al. 2024 (arXiv 2408.10269), Table 1 — OpenCity-plus, zero-shot, PEMS07M: MAE 4.50 / RMSE 8.21 / MAPE 12.20 %), ours 4.5: REPRODUCTION_PASS.
- **PatchSTG, LargeST-SD**, 12-step average MAE: published 16.9, ours 16.9825 (trained with the authors' `main.py`, best epoch 48).
- **TimesFM 2.5:** its own example matches the output it ships, with a largest difference of 7.0e-07.
- **Chronos-Bolt-small:** forecasts reproduced; one MASE value not (rule: within 1 % relative on MASE and WQL); see the investigation in the file.
- **Chronos-2:** no per-dataset Chronos-2 table is published in the repository; nothing to compare against.

**Protocol.**
- **METR-LA and PEMS-BAY:** a chronological 70/10/20 split; per seed (11, 23, 37), a contiguous block of 120 test windows; context 96 steps; every road.
- **Dhaka:** rolling origin over each day, with a one-hour context.
- **Metrics:** BasicTS's masked MAE, RMSE and MAPE (0 = sensor outage), at 15, 30 and 60 minutes. Mean ± sd over seeds; for Dhaka, over the three sites.
- **Unseen city:** trees trained on METR-LA only, on speeds scaled by each window's own context mean, run unchanged on PEMS-BAY and Dhaka, and compared with the same recipe trained at home.
- **Why Dhaka differs:** weekday 06:00-19:00 days; stop-and-go share (steps changing by more than 30 % of free-flow), congested share, coefficient of variation, and autocorrelation at 5-60 minutes.

### The finding

- Los Angeles (METR-LA): at 60 minutes the best is D2STGNN at 3.779 mph mean absolute error, against 6.506 for persistence; the best zero-shot model is Chronos-2, all roads together (zero-shot) at 6.343.
- San Francisco Bay Area (PEMS-BAY): at 60 minutes the best is STID at 1.234 mph mean absolute error, against 2.05 for persistence; the best zero-shot model is Chronos-2, all roads together (zero-shot) at 1.959.
- Dhaka (DhaTraX, three camera sites, one day each), the best model other than persistence: at 15 minutes Chronos-Bolt-small (zero-shot) 2.225 km/h against persistence 2.385 (better); at 30 minutes Chronos-2 (zero-shot) 2.41 km/h against persistence 2.623 (better); at 60 minutes Chronos-2 (zero-shot) 2.877 km/h against persistence 2.717 (not better).
- XGBoost trained on METR-LA (Los Angeles) and run unchanged on PEMS-BAY (San Francisco Bay Area) scores 2.76 mph at 60 minutes, against 2.35 when trained on PEMS-BAY itself: moving cities costs 17.4 %. The six graph models cannot move at all: each is fixed to METR-LA's 207 sensors.

### Why

Dhaka's speeds jump: 6.5-13.1 % of 5-minute steps change by more than 30 % of free-flow, against 0.7 % on a median METR-LA sensor-day and 0.0 % on PEMS-BAY. 2 of 3 sites vary more than a median Los Angeles sensor-day (coefficient of variation BM 0.51, BS 0.19, INT 0.49 against 0.229, and 0.1621 in the Bay Area); 3 of 3 remember less an hour ahead (autocorrelation at 60 minutes 0.08-0.3 against 0.3572 and 0.5766). So what a model learns from Los Angeles's smooth roads does not carry: XGBoost trained on METR-LA is 72.7 % worse than persistence in Dhaka at 60 minutes, while moving it to the Bay Area costs 17.4 %. And the models that win in Los Angeles (D2STGNN 3.779 against persistence 6.506 at 60 minutes) learned months of each sensor's history; a Dhaka camera site has one recorded day, so there is nothing to train them on, and only zero-shot models can be used.

## RQ2 — Forecast-aware closed-loop routing against reactive routing

*Group: ML. City and data: Los Angeles (twin); not run in Dhaka. Summary: `results/v2x/controller_status.json`.*

**Question.** Does forecast-aware closed-loop routing, which reroutes equipped vehicles on predicted rather than current speeds, reduce total travel time against reactive rerouting in the digital twin?

**Data.** The Los Angeles twin (SUMO on OpenStreetMap); the scenario is named by its run folder. Run folder: `results/v2x/closed_loop/la_c4_central_wide_prepeak_ideal_p30_cfgea08dddedc14_stressA_eval_fork1200_x8_peak_arterial` (kept on Google Drive after the storage move; its summary stays here).

**Arms (baselines).** No rerouting (baseline) · reactive rerouting on current speeds · oracle rerouting on the true future speeds · the forecast-aware controller (`predictive_corrected_v2x`); 5 evaluation seeds (201, 202, 203, 204, 205), unseen during tuning; 1236 reroutes applied; forked runs identical up to the fork: True.

**Rule, fixed beforehand.** ESTABLISHED only if vehicle-hours vs baseline AND vs reactive are HELPED (95 % CI excludes zero) on evaluation seeds and teleports are not HURT.

### Results

| Comparison (vehicle-hours, mean over paired seeds) | Result |
|---|---|
| Forecast-aware against no rerouting | -0.12 % (95 % CI -1.07 to +0.83; 2 wins, 3 losses): INCONCLUSIVE |
| Forecast-aware against reactive | -0.91 % (95 % CI -2.15 to +0.33; 4 wins, 1 losses): INCONCLUSIVE |
| Oracle against reactive | -1.83 % (95 % CI -3.52 to -0.14; 5 wins, 0 losses): HELPED |
| Teleports against no rerouting | INCONCLUSIVE |

### The finding

- Future information is worth something: rerouting on the true future speeds beats reactive rerouting (-1.83 % (95 % CI -3.52 to -0.14; 5 wins, 0 losses): HELPED). Status: FUTURE_INFORMATION_VALUE ESTABLISHED.
- Our forecasts do not yet turn that into a traffic benefit: against reactive -0.91 % (95 % CI -2.15 to +0.33; 4 wins, 1 losses): INCONCLUSIVE. Status: TRAFFIC_BENEFIT_NOT_ESTABLISHED.
- Not run in Dhaka: RQ1 found no model with skill an hour ahead there, and the Dhaka twin's demand is not calibrated.

### Why

The oracle arm bounds what any forecast could give on this network. Why the forecast-aware arm falls short of it has not been isolated; every earlier attempt is kept in `results/v2x/controller_status.json` (`history`), none established.

### Threats to validity

- One Los Angeles scenario and one equipped share in the headline run.
- Five evaluation seeds: the confidence intervals are wide.

## RQ3 — V2X ambulance alert and signal control (make-way v2)

*Group: Networking and safety. City and data: Dhaka (simulated, generated demand). Summary: `results/emergency/dhaka/rq2/summary.json`.*

*Result folders and code keep the earlier name RQ2 (`results/emergency/dhaka/rq2/`), because evidence hashes point at them.*

**Question.** How much time does the ambulance alert save, at which traffic densities and equipped shares, against which baselines, and why?

**Data.**
- **Network:** the Dhaka twin's OpenStreetMap network.
- **Demand:** the generated demand (randomTrips, SYNTHETIC_WHAT_IF, not calibrated), scaled by SUMO `--scale`.
- **Trip:** the ambulance's 3.4 km route crosses 2 signals and the DhaTraX INT camera section.

**Arms (baselines).**
- Siren only (SUMO's bluelight behaviour, no message).
- The alert to equipped vehicles, over ITS-G5.
- The alert plus signal preemption (adaptive-traffic-signal-rl, MIT, reproduced earlier).
- **sumo-rl** Q-learning signals (MIT, commit 86f0fc4), with and without the alert. It controls the 3 signals within 500 m of the route, with 30 training episodes per density level on separate seeds, then frozen.

**Reproduced first.** sumo-rl's own `ql_single-intersection.py` against its fixed timing. Q-learning's mean total waiting time over the last 20 % of steps is 31.2 s against fixed timing's 3418365.7 s: pass. The repository publishes no number, so this checks the direction of its README figure. **The fixed-timing comparison is lopsided:** with 42 s greens on this example's routes, the queue under fixed timing grows throughout the run, so the comparison passes trivially. The real evidence is the learning curve: in each of three runs, Q-learning's waiting time falls from 78.9, 73.3, 86.8 s in the first tenth to 28.9, 31.0, 33.6 s in the last fifth.

**Protocol.**
- **Density ladder:** demand ×0.5, ×1, ×2, ×3, ×4, until gridlock (no-alert ambulance not arrived on >= 3 of 5 seeds, or teleports > 5% of vehicles).
- **Equipped shares:** 25, 50, 75 and 100 %.
- **Seeds:** 5 siren-only seeds per level for the gridlock verdict; 10 finished (arm, siren-only) pairs per table cell. A seed is replaced when SUMO's bluelight device crashes.
- **Time saved:** each run against the siren-only run of the same seed and density.
- **Moving aside:** Moving aside (make-way v2): alerted vehicles keep their own route lane changes, and only those within 100 m ahead of the ambulance move aside. No published distance at which drivers act on an electronic alert was found, so 50 m and 100 m were piloted at the Dhaka-density level and chosen by a rule fixed before the pilot (the longer, unless it still produced a circular wait). The rule as fixed rejected both, only on waits that also occur identically in the siren-only runs of the same seeds; the owner amended it after the pilot (a flagged wait counts only if the siren-only run of the same seed does not show it), which gives 100 m. Time saved was not used in the choice.
- **Measures, per run:** free width ahead of the ambulance, the share of alerted vehicles that moved aside, seconds blocked, and queues at the route's signals.

**Dhaka-density row (RQ2b).**
- **Corridor:** the edges within 300 m of the ambulance route (1198 of 6527), cut with SUMO's `netconvert --keep-edges.input-file`, so gridlock on unrelated roads cannot starve the corridor. The route's signal programmes are unchanged by the cut.
- **Demand:** SUMO's `routeSampler.py` matched to DhaTraX's recorded 5-minute counts at INT and BM, 07:00-08:00 (3451 sampled trips plus 937 uncalibrated background trips), the Dhaka class mix, and the smaller lateral gaps for two-wheelers, rickshaws and CNGs (sublane model, 0.8 m).
- **Density reached (INT, no ambulance):** at the full counts, 200.3 vehicles/km (overshoot). Scales tried (median over 5 seeds): x0.4: 9.08; x0.5: 11.23; x0.6: 12.82; x0.7: 15.14; x0.72: 20.3; x0.75: 68.38; x0.78: 54.37; x0.8: 103.08. Chosen x0.78: **54.37 vehicles/km** (seeds' 25-75 %: 42.7-83.7) against the recorded **57.7** (07:00-08:00, 25-75 %: 36.5-126.6); label "Dhaka density".
- **Flow at that density:** 653 vehicles/h through INT against 1184 recorded.
- **Arms:** siren only, alert and alert plus preemption at 25-100 % equipped, Q-learning signals trained 30 episodes on the corridor, with and without the alert; 10 paired seeds each.

### The finding

- With every vehicle equipped, the alert's median saving is -0.5 s (-0.3 %) at demand x0.5; 0.5 s (0.2 %) at demand x1; 2.5 s (1.1 %) at demand x2; 3.5 s (1.2 %) at demand x3; -2.0 s (-0.5 %) at demand x4.
- No equipped share reaches a median saving of 10 % at any density.
- In 70 of 400 alert runs the ambulance was more than 5 s slower than with the siren alone (worst 552 s slower, at demand x4); these runs are kept in every mean, not dropped.
- Q-learning signals without the alert: median saving 11.0 s at x0.5, 32.5 s at x1, 36.0 s at x2, 39.0 s at x3, 92.5 s at x4 against SUMO's own signal programmes (3 signals near the route controlled).
- On the whole network, Dhaka's recorded density at the INT camera (57.7 vehicles/km, 07:00-08:00) is not reached before the simulation gridlocks: the densest run gives 4.54 vehicles/km on that section.
- At the density the corridor reaches (54.37 vehicles/km on the INT section against the recorded 57.7), the alert to every vehicle saves a median 26.0 s (time saved, 25-75 %: -17.0 to 39.8 s) over 10 paired seeds, 4 of them slower than the siren alone; with signal preemption it saves a median 3.0 s (time saved, 25-75 %: -21.0 to 32.5 s); with Q-learning signals and no alert it costs a median 49.5 s (time saved, 25-75 %: -243.8 to 140.0 s). Only 653 vehicles an hour pass the section there, against 1184 recorded: the density matches, the flow does not.

### Why

Time saved falls with the free width ahead of the ambulance in the siren-only run (Spearman rho -0.16, 95 % CI -0.248 to -0.062, 400 paired runs) and rises with the queue at the route's signals (rho 0.179, CI 0.081 to 0.272). It is not clearly related to the share of alerted vehicles that actually moved aside (rho -0.003, CI -0.098 to 0.093). It is not clearly related to how long the siren-only ambulance stood blocked (rho 0.005, CI -0.093 to 0.104). The free width ahead hardly changes with density (7.71-8.42 m over all levels): on this route there is room to move aside at every level, so room is not what limits the alert. Smarter signals do more: the Q-learning signals' median saving beats the alert to every vehicle at 5 of 5 densities, which suggests the ambulance's delay on this route sits at the junctions more than behind vehicles that could move aside.

At the Dhaka-density level, time saved is not clearly related to the free width ahead (rho -0.123, CI -0.367 to 0.129, 80 paired runs), is not clearly related to the queue at the route's signals (rho -0.063, CI -0.291 to 0.169) and rises with how long the siren-only ambulance stood blocked (rho 0.294, CI 0.052 to 0.498; median 86.0 s blocked). Free width ahead averages 7.11 m there (whole network: 7.71-8.42 m).

**Q-learning signals at Dhaka density (inspected).** On the 5 seeds where the Q-learning signals made the ambulance slower at Dhaka density, it stood 2065 s in all (siren-only on the same seeds: 699 s). 0 of those seconds were within 100 m of a traffic light; the next light on its route was green for it in 1962 s, red in 0 s, and there was no light left ahead in 103 s. The queues it stood in were short (median 2 vehicles to the head) and their heads were not at a light; many ended at a vehicle waiting to change lane. So the lights did not hold the ambulance directly. The Q-learning controllers change when traffic is released around the route, which may load the junctions where it stood, but these logs do not show that path; the cause of the slowdown is not established. Details: `evidence/ambulance_rq2/inspect/dhaka/QLEARNING.md`.

**Make-way v1, a record (RQ2b).** The earlier results came from a make-way rule with a circular-wait artefact, now corrected: In the 7 of 10 Dhaka-density runs where the alert to every vehicle made the ambulance slower, it stood 2870 s in all; 63 % of that time it was held by a chain of vehicles ending at an alerted vehicle that waited at the end of a lane it needed to leave. The make-way settings forbid that vehicle's own lane changes until the ambulance has passed it, and the ambulance cannot pass, so both wait until SUMO removes the vehicle after its 300 s teleport limit. A driver would not be expected to wait minutes at a lane end for an ambulance stuck behind them, and the wait ends only through the simulator's teleport, so this part is read as a simulation artefact of how moving aside is applied (our make-way rule, which copies SUMO's bluelight settings and holds them until the ambulance passes). Letting those vehicles keep their own lane changes turns the median into a saving of 37.0 s (25-75 %: -2.5 to 108.5 s, 8 runs, 2 slower, 2 crashed in SUMO); alerting only vehicles within 50 m ahead gives 65.5 s (-15.8 to 270.2 s, 3 of 10 slower); keeping junctions free of make-way changes nothing (median -230.0 s, the same runs). The other 37 % of the time the ambulance stood behind alerted vehicles with no such lane end among the four ahead, or with nothing alerted ahead; whether that is vehicles moving aside blocking each other, or the same wait further ahead, was not resolved. Ten seeds at one density: most of the slowdown at Dhaka density is a simulation artefact, and whether the alert helps there is not known. Those results are kept in `results/emergency/dhaka/rq2/record_v1_makeway_artefact/` and appear only in the old-against-new table.

Why the simulation stays far below Dhaka's recorded density at the INT camera (57.7 vehicles/km, 07:00-08:00): (1) not the definition: both count every vehicle class on the whole road over about 90 m (89.2 m camera zone, 91.8 m simulated section); (2) not the space per vehicle: SUMO's lengths and gaps for this mix allow about 443.3 vehicles/km on those 3 lanes, and other roads in the same run reach 359.1 vehicles/km at gridlock; (3) partly lane discipline: with smaller lateral gaps for two-wheelers, rickshaws and CNGs the section's mean density rises from 4.8 to 24.82 vehicles/km, still below the recorded value; (4) mainly supply: only 260 vehicles an hour enter the section in the simulation, against 1184 recorded, because the generated trips do not load this road and the network gridlocks elsewhere first. So the whole-network ambulance results come from a simulation that does not reach Dhaka's recorded density on the road it measures. (5) Supply tested: a corridor 300 m around the route, loaded with the recorded counts at INT and BM and the smaller lateral gaps, overshoots at the full counts (200.3 vehicles/km on INT) and reaches 54.37 vehicles/km at x0.78 of the counts, inside the recorded range (36.5-126.6); but only 653 vehicles an hour pass, against 1184 recorded. The density is reached; the recorded flow at that density is not.

### Threats to validity

- all traffic is simulated (SUMO); the demand is generated, not calibrated to Dhaka counts
- the ambulance's 3.4 km route crosses 2 signals; the Q-learning baseline controls the 3 signals within 500 m of it
- ITS-G5 only in the sweep; C-V2X and 4G at one density are in the earlier table
- SUMO 1.27.1's bluelight device crashes on some seeds; a crashed seed is replaced by the next one
- the simulation does not reach Dhaka's recorded density on the INT section (see density_gap): every ambulance number here is from traffic lighter on that road than Dhaka's recorded morning, and is not a statement about Dhaka at its recorded density
- Dhaka-density row: a corridor cut 300 m around the route (1198 of 6527 roads), with no traffic from the rest of the city
- Dhaka-density row: one recorded day per camera (INT 2026-04-19, BM 2026-04-20), two different days merged into one 07:00-08:00 window
- Dhaka-density row: the density matches (54.37 vs 57.7 vehicles/km) but the flow does not (653 vs 1184 vehicles/h): at the same density, traffic on the simulated section moves slower than the recorded traffic
- Dhaka-density row: at the full recorded counts the corridor overshoots (200.3 vehicles/km on INT, 597 vehicles/h through); the counts are scaled to x0.78 to match the density, and the density reached does not rise smoothly with the scale (see tried scales)
- Dhaka-density row: the route's signal programmes are unchanged by the cut
- every alerting arm uses make-way v2 (configs/ambulance.yaml): alerted vehicles keep their own route lane changes and only those within 100 m ahead move aside; no published distance for an electronic alert exists, so 50 and 100 m were piloted and chosen by a rule fixed beforehand and amended once by the owner after the pilot (evidence/ambulance_rq2/makeway_v2/choice.json)
- the make-way v1 results (a circular-wait artefact) are kept as a labelled record in record_v1_makeway_artefact/ and appear only in the old-vs-new table

## RQ4 — Offline phone-to-phone mesh (Epidemic, Spray and Wait, PRoPHET)

*Group: Networking and safety. City and data: Dhaka (simulated, sparse setting). Summary: `evidence/mesh/dhaka/results.json`.*

**Question.** When the mobile network is down, how many alerts reach other vehicles phone to phone, how fast, and at what cost in copies, with Epidemic, Spray and Wait and PRoPHET?

**Data.** Dhaka's SUMO network (`data/cities/dhaka_r1/dhaka_r1.net.xml.gz`), demand SYNTHETIC randomTrips, UNCALIBRATED, seed 11; Eclipse SUMO sumo 1.27.1.

**Simulator.** pons-dtn 0.2.1 (PONS, MIT; `third_party/SOURCES.md`), routers at PONS defaults. Radio BT_10m, range 10 m (The ONE simulator, default_settings.txt, lines 19-23: "# \"B…).

**Protocol.** Window 900-1200 s; 17 of 277 vehicles present throughout take part; one 100-byte message every 1 s, TTL 300 s; seeds [1, 2, 3, 4, 5].

**Reproduced first.** PONS publishes no reference numbers for this setting; it is used as released (`research/mesh_pons/RESULTS.md`).

only the 17 of 277 vehicles present for the whole window take part, so a message reaches only those; low delivery is the sparse setup, not a property of the city

### Threats to validity

## RQ5 — Weak-signal prefetch: time to a usable route and radio energy (Bartendr, BreadCrumbs)

*Group: Networking and safety. City and data: Dhaka route, emulated network. Summary: `evidence/prefetch/summary.json`.*

**Question.** Does fetching the route pack while the signal is good (BreadCrumbs) and scheduling downloads into strong-signal stretches (Bartendr) shorten the time to a usable route and save radio energy on a weak link?

**Network.** emulated in headless Chromium (CDP); presets fast 4G 4/3 Mbit/s 20 ms, slow 3G 400/400 kbit/s 400 ms, 2G-like 50/20 kbit/s 1000 ms, offline 10 s after the route

**Protocol.** 24 runs in headless Chromium; route pack median 10201674 bytes. Energy: estimated from Bartendr's Palm Pre measurements (ACM MobiCom 2010), not measured on our phones; SYNTHETIC: weak stretches over 10, 30 and 50 % of the real Dhaka route, 3 seeds each, one 200 m tunnel

**References.** Bartendr: Schulman, Navda, Ramjee, Spring, ACM MobiCom 2010; BreadCrumbs: Nicholson, Noble, ACM MobiCom 2008; Borkowski, Skarlat, Schulte, Dustdar, IEEE MS 2016 (secondary).

### Results (SIMULATED network conditions)

| Condition | Arm | Route shown | Median time to route, s | Median MB |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2g_like | without_prefetch | 3 of 3 | 7.4 | 0.031 |
| 2g_like | with_prefetch | 3 of 3 | 0.8 | 0.0 |
| fast_4g | without_prefetch | 3 of 3 | 0.8 | 0.031 |
| fast_4g | with_prefetch | 3 of 3 | 0.8 | 0.001 |
| offline_mid_route | without_prefetch | 3 of 3 | 0.8 | 0.056 |
| offline_mid_route | with_prefetch | 3 of 3 | 0.8 | 0.001 |
| slow_3g | without_prefetch | 3 of 3 | 1.8 | 0.03 |
| slow_3g | with_prefetch | 3 of 3 | 0.8 | 0.0 |

| Weak share of route | Seed | Now, J | As needed, J | Bartendr, J | Saving against as needed, % |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.1 | 1 | 35.1 | 62.0 | 35.1 | 43.4 |
| 0.1 | 2 | 35.1 | 62.6 | 35.1 | 43.9 |
| 0.1 | 3 | 35.1 | 72.1 | 35.1 | 51.3 |
| 0.3 | 1 | 35.1 | 97.6 | 35.1 | 64.0 |
| 0.3 | 2 | 35.1 | 104.9 | 35.1 | 66.5 |
| 0.3 | 3 | 35.1 | 106.9 | 35.1 | 67.2 |

### The finding

- On a 2G-like link the route appears in 0.8 s with the pack against 7.4 s without; on fast 4G there is no difference.
- Prefetching the pack while the signal is good is estimated to save 43.4-73.3 % of radio energy against downloading as needed, more the weaker the route.
- Bartendr's schedule adds no extra saving in our runs: in 9 of 9 rows it chose a single download at once, the same energy as downloading the pack now.

### Why

A weak radio sends slowly and stays powered longer per byte (Bartendr's measurement); fetching once in a strong stretch avoids both.

### Threats to validity

- Energy is estimated from Bartendr's Palm Pre measurements (ACM MobiCom 2010), not measured on our phones.
- strong = the fast-4G preset (4 Mbit/s); medium and weak at Bartendr's measured throughput ratios (1/2 and 1/4), not the slower emulated presets, so the estimate stays within what the paper measured
