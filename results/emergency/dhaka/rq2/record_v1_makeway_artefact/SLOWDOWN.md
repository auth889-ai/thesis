# Why the alert slows the ambulance at Dhaka density (RQ2b)

Generated by `research/ambulance_rq2/scripts/slowdown_summary.py` from the logs in this folder.

## Reading

In the 7 of 10 Dhaka-density runs where the alert to every vehicle made the ambulance slower, it stood 2870 s in all; 63 % of that time it was held by a chain of vehicles ending at an alerted vehicle that waited at the end of a lane it needed to leave. The make-way settings forbid that vehicle's own lane changes until the ambulance has passed it, and the ambulance cannot pass, so both wait until SUMO removes the vehicle after its 300 s teleport limit. A driver would not be expected to wait minutes at a lane end for an ambulance stuck behind them, and the wait ends only through the simulator's teleport, so this part is read as a simulation artefact of how moving aside is applied (our make-way rule, which copies SUMO's bluelight settings and holds them until the ambulance passes). Letting those vehicles keep their own lane changes turns the median into a saving of 37.0 s (25-75 %: -2.5 to 108.5 s, 8 runs, 2 slower, 2 crashed in SUMO); alerting only vehicles within 50 m ahead gives 65.5 s (-15.8 to 270.2 s, 3 of 10 slower); keeping junctions free of make-way changes nothing (median -230.0 s, the same runs). The other 37 % of the time the ambulance stood behind alerted vehicles with no such lane end among the four ahead, or with nothing alerted ahead; whether that is vehicles moving aside blocking each other, or the same wait further ahead, was not resolved. Ten seeds at one density: most of the slowdown at Dhaka density is a simulation artefact, and whether the alert helps there is not known.
